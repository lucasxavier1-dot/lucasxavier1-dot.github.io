In this monograph, we investigate an LC circuit coupled to a superconducting qubit. This device forms the foundation of quantum computers based on superconducting qubits—a technology used by companies such as Google and IBM (15; 16). Driven by the scientific and commercial challenge of moving beyond classical computing, quantum computers will be capable of solving specific problems more efficiently than classical ones. A well-established race to develop the best quantum computer is already underway among various companies worldwide, a competition that relies heavily on advancements in qubit control, measurement, and quality.

Superconducting qubits and quantum resonators are essential components of quantum computers, and the field dedicated to studying them is circuit quantum electrodynamics (cQED). We begin by introducing classical and quantum LC circuits, followed by the qubit formed by the parallel combination of a Josephson junction and a capacitor. Subsequently, we demonstrate that the dynamics of a qubit coupled to an LC circuit are described by the Jaynes-Cummings model.

A microwave resonator behaves as a quantum LC circuit and can be utilized for qubit readout and control. We show that the Jaynes-Cummings model emerges naturally from the derivation of the Hamiltonian of a transmon coupled to a microwave resonator.

In Chapter 2, we study the LC circuit. In Chapter 3, we address the Josephson junction in the transmon regime. In Chapter 4, we examine the interaction between the transmon and a quantum resonator, deriving the Jaynes-Cummings Hamiltonian. Finally, our conclusions are presented in Chapter 5.
